# AEGIS benchmark report

Run on 18 November 2026 in `benchmark/sol`, starting from the clean starter at `8b14fb800825eb25bc77785184aecaf8022c5f82`.

## Delivered

- **Engineering Hangar.** An original procedural Kestrel XR-07 with a faceted chassis, ducted animated rotors, structural arms, skids and a gimbal. The bay includes a cutaway ceiling, loading door, utility equipment, local lighting and an illuminated inspection platform. The operator can orbit, zoom, choose Hero, Side or Top, and configure finish, lights and engine.
- **Flight Range.** A three-second countdown leads into a 60-second trial. The airframe accelerates, drifts, banks and responds to lift and boost with profile-specific thrust and energy draw. Swept collisions cover cargo, gate frames and range boundaries. Each gate requires a forward pass through its opening, in order. The run can pause, reset, time out, finish or replay.
- **Mission Control.** Speed, altitude and energy are sampled from the simulator. The chart supports point selection by pointer, touch and keyboard. Recorded runs retain their configuration, score, outcome and samples. The local archive and leaderboard read from SQLite and expose loading, empty and error states.
- **Benchmark views.** Five deterministic captures are included. All 3D geometry, particles, materials, markings and lighting are produced locally in code. The flight capture uses an initial maneuver generated by the actual simulator. Mission Control uses a clearly labeled demo archive for the presentation URL.

All requested modules and artifacts are present. The mobile pass used Chromium emulation and physical handset behavior remains unverified.

## Engineering decisions

- Flight physics runs at a fixed 120 Hz with an accumulator. The Three.js frame loop reads the full state, while React receives instrument updates at 10 Hz and immediately on gates, impacts and state changes. The launch configuration is locked for the run. Navigating away from an active trial pauses it.
- Scoring, validation and the scene share the gate and obstacle geometry. Validation checks ranges, chronology, telemetry endpoints, outcome and score consistency. UUID-based persistence makes repeated submissions safe and rejects a conflicting payload for the same run.
- The viewport uses instanced batches for repeated rigid parts, shared geometry and materials, disposal of generated textures and instances, and a bounded particle budget. HIGH uses a 2048 shadow map, antialiasing, and DPR 1.5. MEDIUM uses a 1024 shadow map and capped DPR. LOW uses DPR 1 and omits dynamic shadows and particles.
- The scene modules load on demand. The chart and archive operate without the 3D bundle. The UI uses locally packaged typefaces, a graphite instrument console, cyan navigational highlighting and amber for the current gate and primary action.

## Limitations and known behavior

- The simulator models thrust, drag and bounded motion with a simple collision envelope. The mission clock follows simulated time. A render stall over 250 ms can make it advance more slowly than wall time, and time pauses when the pilot leaves the flight view.
- The leaderboard is designed for local use and accepts validated reports from an unauthenticated client. Results can be fabricated by a client that satisfies those checks.
- A save failure can be retried from the finished run or Mission Control within the current session. The airframe selection is retained across modules during that session.
- The full dependency install reported 16 high-severity audit entries with dev dependencies included. The production-only npm audit reported zero findings at verification time.
- The development renderer emitted an upstream Three.js `Clock` deprecation warning on the pinned React Three Fiber version.
- The goal tracker reported 265,558 aggregate tokens and 2,479 elapsed seconds when measurement was recorded. Provider billing and a token-category breakdown were unavailable.
